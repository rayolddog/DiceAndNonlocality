# DiceAndNonlocality

Max Born wrote a letter to Albert Einstein In November of 1926.  A rough translation of part of the letter is,
 “my thought of conceiving Schrödinger’s wave field as a ‘ghost field’ in your sense is increasingly proving its worth.”
 Continuing, Born wrote, “:The probability field of course does not move in ordinary space, but in phase—or rather, in configuration space”.  Einstein’s returned a letter in German with a rough translation, "The theory says a lot, but does not really bring us any closer to the secret of the ‘Old One’. I, at any rate, am convinced that He is not playing at dice.  Waves in 3n-dimensional space whose velocity is regulated by potential energy (for example, rubber bands)… The business with Schrödinger’s equations together with Born’s interpretation of the ∣ψ∣-function does not yet seem to me to be final, even though one must admire the accomplishment as a step toward the truth." In the Fifth Sovay Conference of 1927, Einstein continued the argument that quantum mechanics was not a complete theory. The proposed role of probability and configuration space led to the famous argument between Neils Bohr and Albert Einstein at the 1927 Solvay Conference.  In the later paper published in 1935, ““Can Quantum-Mechanical Description of Physical Reality Be Considered Complete?”, with authors Einstein. Poldolsky, and Rosen, the issue was raised that entanglement required non locality. The purpose of this work is to outline a possible mechanism for recording individual events in a quantum experiment resulting in a distribution of observations consistent with the Born rule. In another paper, a mechanism for entanglement is proposed.  Basically, the wave packet is real and localized, time is nonlocal.

The Born rule for calculating the results of quantum interactions continued to be used successfully for quantum experiments.  In Feynman’s recipe for calculating results of quantum interactions, interference of complex waves is translated into distributions of probability using the Born rule, and a random number generator for selecting an individual event of interaction from the Born rule distribution of probability would be used to simulate individual events accumulating into a distribution compatible with the Born rule..  QED theory is one of the most successful quantum field theories and made use of the Born rule.

For decades, several derivations been made to derive the Born rule, but none have been universally accepted.  This work with the help of AI does not try to derive the Born rule. The Born rule is useful to calculate the interference pattern of interacting complex waves.  This is a purely geometric interpretation of the Born rule, an accepted complex geometric rule.  We propose an interpretation of the wave interference as describing the energy distribution interacting with a detector. The  mechanism in this manuscript replaces configuration space with a physically motivated mechanism. Randomness enters into the calculation from the random distribution of phases in the condensed matter of the detector, and/or randomness in the quantum vacuum.  Born selection is modeled with an energy conserving martingale with drift that accumulates in an equivalent Born rule distribution.  The proposed mechanism also allows for recent experiments demonstrating reversibility of the measurement process early in the process of interaction.  One advantage of the proposed mechanism is the possibility of reversibility in the early stages of measurement.  The mechanism favors Einstein’s objection that quantum theory was incomplete. What this paper will attempt is to replace the random number generator with a physically based source on unbiased noise.
